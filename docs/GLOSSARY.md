# Glossary

## Actual Size / 100%
Print without intentionally scaling the document. Downstream drivers can still interfere, which is why Print Truth/calibration matter.

## ArtBox
PDF box describing a meaningful art/content region. Usage varies.

## Bleed
Artwork extending beyond the final trim edge so cutting does not expose an unwanted white edge.

## BleedBox
PDF box representing the region including bleed.

## Borderless
Printer/media mode intended to print to/over the physical edge. It is a hardware/media capability, not the same as adding bleed.

## Calibration
Measured correction for repeatable printer/transport/media deviation.

## Creep
Progressive displacement of inner pages in nested folded sheets due to paper thickness.

## Crop marks / trim marks
Marks showing where a printed piece should be cut.

## CropBox
PDF box used by viewers/printing to define the displayed/printed region.

## Cut-and-stack
Imposition that arranges pages so stacks created by cutting can be placed together in correct sequence.

## DNS-SD
DNS-Based Service Discovery; commonly used with mDNS for finding printers/services.

## DPI
Dots per inch, often used for printer device resolution.

## Effective PPI
Raster pixels per inch after an image is placed/scaled to its final physical dimensions.

## Finishing
Post-print operations such as cutting, folding, stapling, binding, punching, laminating.

## Gang / ganging
Placing multiple different jobs/items on the same sheet/roll to improve utilization.

## Guillotine cut
Straight cut that passes through the sheet/stack; layouts can be optimized to reduce the number/complexity of these cuts.

## Hardware margin
Physical region near a sheet edge a printer cannot mark under the current conditions.

## Imposition
Arrangement of logical pages/items on physical sheets for printing, folding, cutting, and binding.

## IPP
Internet Printing Protocol.

## IPP Everywhere
Printer Working Group driverless printing ecosystem based on IPP, discovery, and standard document/raster formats.

## MediaBox
PDF box defining the full physical page medium.

## N-up
Multiple logical pages/items placed on one physical sheet side.

## Overprint
Print behavior where one color/object prints over another rather than knocking it out; important in professional prepress.

## PDF/X
Family of PDF standards aimed at reliable graphic-arts/print exchange.

## PPI
Pixels per inch for raster image sampling.

## Preflight
Checking a job against production requirements before printing.

## Printable region
Area a printer is expected to mark for a selected media/configuration.

## PrintPlan
Print Studio’s resolved digital production intent before submission.

## PrintJob
A submitted/executing physical print attempt.

## Print Truth
Print Studio concept summarizing effective physical output, scaling, printer constraints, calibration, and warnings.

## PWG Raster
Standard raster format used in modern driverless printing workflows.

## Recipe
Reusable production intent: media, constraints, layout strategy, copies, marks, duplex, output preferences.

## Registration
Alignment of front/back, separations, colors, or finishing operations.

## Registration marks
Marks used to align production processes.

## Safe area
Region where important content should remain to tolerate trim/production variation.

## Saddle stitch
Folded sheets nested and stapled through the center fold.

## Signature
A group of pages imposed on sheets, folded/gathered as a unit in binding.

## Slug
Non-final production area containing notes/identification/marks.

## Smart Layout
Print Studio solver that creates explainable physical arrangements from constraints and optimization objectives.

## Step-and-repeat
Repeated copies of the same item/page across a sheet.

## Trim
Final cut boundary/finished page edge.

## TrimBox
PDF box representing intended finished dimensions.

## Work-and-tumble
Two-sided production method involving flipping the sheet end-over-end.

## Work-and-turn
Two-sided production method involving turning the sheet left-to-right while using a common plate/setup.

## URF
Apple raster format used in AirPrint-related workflows.

## VDP
Variable Data Printing — each copy can contain different text/images/barcodes driven by records.
